Lee Kuan Yew (李光耀)

Founding Father & First Prime Minister of Modern Singapore
16 September 1923 – 23 March 2015

The visionary statesman who spearheaded Singapore's transformation from a vulnerable third-world island nation into one of the world's most prosperous, safe, and technologically advanced global metropolises.

⚡ Famous Words & Philosophy
"Even from my sick bed, even if you are going to lower me into the grave and I feel that something is going wrong, I will get up."
Lee Kuan Yew 1988 National Day Rally

Biography & Journey

Lee Kuan Yew was born in Singapore in 1923. An exceptionally brilliant scholar, he survived the hardship of the Japanese Occupation during World War II before pursuing law at Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge, where he graduated with a rare Starred First-Class Honours.

In 1954, he co-founded the People's Action Party (PAP) and became Singapore’s first Prime Minister in 1959 when the island achieved self-governance. Following a brief and turbulent merger with Malaysia, Singapore was expelled and thrust into sudden, unchosen independence on 9 August 1965.

Confronted with high unemployment, severe housing shortages, and no natural resources, Lee and his founding team enacted radical policies: building a multi-ethnic national identity, world-class public housing (HDB), uncompromising anti-corruption standards (CPIB), and an export-driven open economy that successfully bridged Singapore "From Third World to First".

Interesting Facts

🌳

The "Garden City" Vision

In 1963, he launched the nationwide Tree Planting Campaign. He believed green canopy and cleanliness were crucial signals to international investors of a disciplined, well-run nation.

🗣️

Bilingualism Policy

He made English the unifying working language for commerce and inter-racial cohesion, while mandating that every citizen learn their mother tongue to retain cultural heritage.

🏃

Daily Jogging Discipline

Renowned for extraordinary personal discipline, he maintained a strict daily regimen of brisk walking and running every evening well into his 80s to maintain mental and physical sharpness.

✈️

Bold Changi Airport Move

In 1975, he rejected foreign advice to expand the existing Paya Lebar airport and instead boldly moved Singapore's aviation hub to Changi, enabling decades of unconstrained expansion.

⚖️

Zero Tolerance on Corruption

He established an incorruptible civil service by empowering the CPIB and paying competitive market wages to public officials, making Singapore one of the least corrupt nations globally.

🌏

Counsel to World Leaders

He was sought after for candid geopolitical insights by multiple generations of global leaders, including US Presidents, Chinese leaders from Deng Xiaoping onwards, and European premiers.
